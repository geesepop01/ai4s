# Scientific Brainstorming

Brainstorming is divergent before it is convergent. The quality of the idea
space depends on how far apart the candidates are before you start filtering.

## The two phases

**Phase 1 — Divergent.** Generate many candidates without judging them. No
criticism, no feasibility filter, no "but we don't have the data."

**Phase 2 — Convergent.** Filter and rank using explicit criteria. This is a
separate pass, and it is where feasibility belongs.

Mixing the phases produces safe, incremental ideas and kills the interesting
ones early.

## Divergent techniques

1. **Constraint inversion.** Take an assumption of the field and remove it. What
   becomes possible?
2. **Cross-domain transfer.** What does field A do that field B has not applied?
   (The most reliable source of genuinely new ideas.)
3. **Anomaly chasing.** What result in the literature does not fit? Those are
   where new explanations live.
4. **Scale shift.** What changes if the study is 100× larger, 100× smaller, 10×
   faster, or 10× longer?
5. **Method swap.** Same question, different instrument. What becomes visible?
6. **Boundary conditions.** Where does the current model break? Those boundaries
   are research questions.
7. **Negative space.** What is systematically not studied? Small samples, rare
   populations, failure cases, negative results.
8. **The 5-year question.** If the field achieves its current goals, what becomes
   the next problem? Start there instead.

## The idea record

```markdown
## Idea <n>: <one line>

What is new: <the delta from what exists>
Why now: <what changed — data, method, theory — that makes this possible>
Test: <the smallest experiment that would tell you if this is real>
Failure mode: <how this idea dies>
Cost: <rough, in time and resources>
Dependencies: <what you need that you do not have>
```

## Convergent criteria

Score each surviving idea (1–5) on:

- **Novelty** — is the delta real, or a repackaging?
- **Importance** — does answering this change what anyone does?
- **Testability** — is there a decisive experiment feasible in your constraints?
- **Feasibility** — data, methods, skills, compute, time.
- **Risk/reward** — high-risk/high-reward vs safe/incremental. Deliberately keep
  some of each.

Rank and present **three tiers**: the safe deliverable, the ambitious bet, and
the long shot. A portfolio beats a single pick.

## Cross-field ideas specifically

When a question spans fields (e.g. pediatric audio + developmental neuroscience +
signal processing):

- Enumerate what each field would say the question is. The disagreements are the
  interesting part.
- Identify which field's methods are being borrowed and whether the borrowing is
  legitimate or superficial.
- Check whether the borrowed method's assumptions hold in the new setting.

## Anti-patterns

- Brainstorming by listing what is already in the literature.
- Filtering during generation.
- One idea, treated as a plan, before any alternatives existed.
- Ignoring feasibility entirely and producing only impossible ideas.
- Keeping only the idea that matches the current skillset.
