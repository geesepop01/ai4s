# Scientific Slides

Slides are not a document projected on a wall. They are a timed, visual,
spoken-over medium. The slide carries the visual; you carry the words.

## Structure of a research talk

| Segment | Time share | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | ~10% | The question in one sentence and why it matters |
| Background | ~15% | Only what the audience needs to follow the result |
| Question & approach | ~15% | The hypothesis and the design, at the level of logic |
| Results | ~35% | The findings, one per slide, with the visual that shows them |
| Interpretation | ~15% | What it means, what it does not mean |
| Take-home | ~10% | One sentence the audience remembers |

## One idea per slide

A slide that carries two findings carries neither. If a slide needs two
sentences to explain its title, it is two slides.

- **Title is the finding**, not the topic. "Accuracy improves with augmentation"
  beats "Augmentation results."
- **Body text is minimal.** If the audience is reading, they are not listening.
- **The visual is the argument.** A figure that needs a paragraph of explanation
  is the wrong figure for a slide.

## Visual rules

- **Type size:** 24pt minimum body, 32pt+ for titles. If it does not read from
  the back of the room, it is too small.
- **High contrast.** Dark text on light background, or light on dark. Projectors
  wash out mid-tones and subtle color.
- **Colorblind-safe palettes**, and never rely on color alone to distinguish
  series — add markers or line styles.
- **One plot per slide** where possible. Multi-panel figures are for papers.
- **Label the axes with units**, and state n and the uncertainty in the caption.
- **No paragraphs. No full sentences where a phrase works.**

## Building the deck

**Beamer (LaTeX):** consistent with a paper's notation; use a clean theme, not
the default with its navigation symbols. `\pause` sparingly; animate the build of
one figure rather than showing five versions.

**PowerPoint/Keynote:** use the slide master for consistency; align to a grid;
avoid transitions and animations except progressive reveal of one figure.

**Generated decks (python-pptx, pptxgenjs):** good for data-driven decks — slides
from a template plus figures generated by script. Keep the generation script with
the figures so the deck is reproducible.

## Rehearse against the clock

- Time the talk with the actual slides. A 12-minute slot means 11 minutes of
  content and a margin.
- Cut the backup slides first, then the background, then the secondary results.
  Never cut the take-home.
- Prepare 2–3 backup slides for likely questions: the extra control, the
  alternative analysis, the limitations in detail.

## Before delivering

- [ ] Every figure is readable at the size it will be projected.
- [ ] Every number on a slide traces to a result file (see `deliverable-conformance`).
- [ ] The deck compiles/exports cleanly; no broken references or missing fonts.
- [ ] The take-home sentence appears on the last slide.
- [ ] The talk fits the slot with a margin.
